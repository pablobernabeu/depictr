# Simulated wellbeing survey

A reproducibly simulated cross-sectional wellbeing survey for
descriptive, correlation, regression and missing-data examples.
`life_satisfaction` depends on its predictors: higher stress lowers it,
more sleep and exercise raise it, log-income has a positive effect of
about +0.45 per natural-log unit, and age has a mild inverted-U effect
that peaks in mid-life. `education` is an ordered factor. Income is
missing more often at higher stress (missing at random), and because
income predicts the outcome, this missingness is informative for the
missing-data examples.

## Usage

``` r
wellbeing_survey
```

## Format

A data frame with 300 rows and 9 variables:

- id:

  Respondent identifier.

- region:

  Region of residence: `North`, `South`, `East` or `West`.

- age:

  Age in years.

- education:

  Highest education level (ordered factor: `secondary` \<
  `undergraduate` \< `postgraduate`).

- income:

  Annual income, with missing values, more of them at high stress.

- stress:

  Self-reported stress (1-7).

- sleep_hours:

  Typical hours of sleep per night, with missing values.

- exercise_days:

  Days of exercise per week (0-7), with missing values.

- life_satisfaction:

  Life satisfaction (1-7), with missing values.

## Source

Simulated by `data-raw/generate_datasets.R` in the source repository.

## Details

The data are synthetic, generated by `data-raw/generate_datasets.R` with
a fixed seed, and they describe no real individuals.
